TrueSight: Simulating AR Displays for Design Evaluation

September 9, 2026

Designers working on Meta’s AR displays often had to make visual decisions without representative hardware. Standard monitors could not reproduce the display’s optical behavior, while test beds and development devices were scarce. Decisions about color, density, typography, and detail were often reopened once the work reached hardware.

Closing the evaluation gap

The problem was not that our design files lacked fidelity. They could be precise representations of the wrong viewing condition.

I created TrueSight, a Mac application that rendered design artifacts through a simulation based on Meta’s display fabrication and evaluation pipeline. The goal was not a perfect digital twin, but to expose the display characteristics that materially affected design judgment early enough to influence the work.

Designers could take artifacts from Figma and compare the source with a closer approximation of the target display without changing their normal workflow.

Using simulation to make decisions

We used TrueSight to evaluate dead-pixel density thresholds, assess display quality, and lock treatments such as WhatsApp brand color. It did not replace hardware testing; it moved routine visual evaluation earlier and reserved scarce hardware for questions that genuinely required it.

Outcome

TrueSight became the primary tool teams used to evaluate AR display quality before hardware access. Its value was not only speed. It changed where confidence could enter the design process, allowing teams to identify display-related problems while decisions were still inexpensive to revisit.
