Google Lens: Unified Answers and Responsive Design

September 9, 2026

Google Lens lets people search using an image rather than starting with text. My work covered two related problems: making identification results clearer despite variable recognition quality, and creating a responsive framework that could scale Lens across devices.

Unified Answers

At the time, Lens surfaced a correct identification answer for only about 15% of queries. Useful information often existed in the results, but it was scattered across multiple components and users could miss it.

The Unified Answer framework defined what Lens should present as an answer: a title, first-position placement, an image that lets the user verify the identification, and a clear path into Search.

That transition introduced practical constraints. The Search action needed to remain visible even when the results panel was partially expanded. We chose “Search” over “Learn more” because answer titles averaged roughly 47 characters and commonly wrapped to two lines.

In prototype research, the design achieved 100% task completion and reported user satisfaction. The work increased the rate at which Lens surfaced correct results by roughly six times and unified four previously separate components.

Aqua

Lens also needed to expand across larger displays, orientations, and new touchpoints. Aqua established a device-agnostic framework rather than allowing each surface to solve the same structural problems independently.

A UI audit showed that three primary screen structures represented roughly 90% of the design problems. One key question was how to divide wider screens between the query image and results. I wrote a script to analyze the aspect-ratio distribution of images people were using with Lens, which informed a wider query-image treatment optimized for about 80% of observed images.

The results panel was bounded between 320dp and 768dp and docked on wider displays. Related grid and breakpoint rules let the experience adapt across phones, tablets, desktop, orientations, and split-screen states.

Unified Answers created structure around variable recognition quality. Aqua created structure around variable device and surface constraints.
