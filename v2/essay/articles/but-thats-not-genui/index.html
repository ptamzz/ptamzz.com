But that’s not GenUI!

August 28, 2026

AI can generate compelling interfaces, but it is not a reliable designer yet. It struggles to sustain the intended craft bar, follow strict interface rules, and is prone to a brand new category of prompt injection that targets the Design itself.

An interface embodies multiple design decisions (eg. IA, hierarchy, layout, composition, visual design etc.). Each of these decisions can independently be generative or deterministic. For instance, AI can generate the layout while the information architecture remains deterministic.

Image generation already works this way. LoRAs, ControlNet, segmented masks, identity preservation, all can constrain style, pose, structure, or a specific aspect of the image while the final image remains generative.

With GenUI, a design.md can constrain style, structured determinism can lock specific content, UI forms & shapes. These constraints do not make the output less generative; they define where generation has authority.

The GenUI Authority Class defines how much authority AI has in the GenUI pipeline

From left to right, design authority shifts progressively from humans to AI, with AI taking on more of the UI-generation pipeline at each class. But more authority is not inherently better. The right class is the one at which the system can consistently meet its intended bar for craft, safety, and reliability—not necessarily the highest class it can technically reach.

Today, our system may operate at C2. As models & the harness improves, we should expand the authority class deliberately, using evaluations and explicit exit criteria to determine when the next class is ready. Declaring the class establishes an operating contract: what AI is trusted to decide today, what remains constrained, and what evidence is required before that authority expands.

GenUI is not about avoiding determinism. It is about assigning generative authority deliberately across the design process—and expanding that authority only where the system has earned it.
